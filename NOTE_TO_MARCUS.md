# Note to Marcus

**What the tool shows you.** Open Corvane Signal on Monday and the first screen answers your three questions. Are we winning or losing in AI? Against whom? What should we do? It shows a one-line verdict, a score for Corvane and for Trakvia, Routelyne and Gridwell, the questions where we lost or gained ground and who took it, every place an AI engine states something false about us, and up to five concrete actions. Priya's pages behind it show every question, every engine and the exact answer the AI gave, with each company highlighted. She can download a board-ready Excel file.

**How the score works.** Every time ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overview answers a buyer question, we give each company points:

- **100** if the answer recommends it
- **50** if it just names it
- **25** if it criticises it
- **0** if it advises against it or leaves it out

Being named first counts fully. Being named later counts a bit less. The score is the average over all the answers we collected, with your most important buyer questions counting three times as much as the least important. So "34/100" means that, out of all the attention the AI hands out, Corvane is getting about a third of the best possible, and you can track that one number every week.

**Why it moved, honestly.** AI engines give different answers when you ask the same question twice. That alone moves a weekly score by about ±10 points. So the tool only calls a change real when it's bigger than what re-asking normally produces, and otherwise labels it "normal variation". It also never compares a full week with an incomplete one. Week 5 had no Perplexity answers, so that week is compared only on the questions and engines collected in both weeks. That avoids a false drop.

**What it says today.**
- **The weekly moves are noise.** On a single week-to-week basis, nobody's change is clearly real.
- **The three-week view is clear.** Weeks 4–6 compared with weeks 1–3 show **a real decline for Corvane (about −7 points)**, mostly on the field-service and pricing questions. **Routelyne is the one gaining (+12).** Trakvia now leads on score.
- **AI engines are spreading false facts about us.** They say we're headquartered in Chicago or in Columbus, Georgia, that we were founded in 2009, that we charge $45–49, that we lack ELD compliance or QuickBooks integration, and that we sell dashcams.
- **Fix those first.** Make the facts unmissable on corvanefleet.com and on the review sites the engines cite, and brief sales so they're ready when a prospect repeats them.

**Why I chose this score.** It rewards what actually wins deals, being recommended and named first, rather than merely being mentioned. It's simple enough to explain in one breath. It's honest about uncertainty, which matters more than precision when the engines themselves are inconsistent.
